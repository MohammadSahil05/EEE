  <section id="components" class="section">
    <h2>Basic Electronic Components</h2>
    <p class="section-subtitle">
      Get familiar with the building blocks of every electronic circuit.
    </p>

    <div class="cards-grid">
      <div class="card">
        <h3>Resistor</h3>
        <p>
          A resistor limits the flow of electric current in a circuit. It is used
          to control voltage and current levels.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Symbol: Zig-zag or rectangle</li>
          <li>Unit: Ohm (Ω)</li>
          <li>Use: Current limiting, voltage divider</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div class="card">
        <h3>Capacitor</h3>
        <p>
          A capacitor stores electrical energy in the form of an electric field
          and releases it when required.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Symbol: Two parallel plates</li>
          <li>Unit: Farad (F)</li>
          <li>Use: Filtering, timing, coupling</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div class="card">
        <h3>Diode</h3>
        <p>
          A diode allows current to flow only in one direction. It acts like an
          electrical one-way valve.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Symbol: Triangle with a line</li>
          <li>Use: Rectification, protection</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div class="card">
        <h3>Transistor</h3>
        <p>
          A transistor works as a switch or amplifier for electronic signals. It’s
          the heart of modern electronics.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Types: NPN, PNP, MOSFET</li>
          <li>Use: Switching, amplification</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

      <div class="card">
        <h3>Integrated Circuit (IC)</h3>
        <p>
          An IC is a complete mini-circuit built on a small chip containing
          many transistors, resistors, etc.
        </p>
        <ul>
          <li>Use: Microcontrollers, op-amps, timers</li>
        </ul>
      </div>
    </div>
  </section>
